# Regime: orbital bodies

Anything falling around something else: planets, moons, a satellite, a thrown object.

The textbook case of one root quantity.

## Must be known before drawing

| # | question | the measured defect that put it here |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Kepler III: the period FOLLOWS from the semi-major axis.** | `T = a^1.5`. A 25x span of distance is a 123x span of period, so inner bodies lap outer ones many times — the thing that makes an orrery read. The old scene chose periods to look nice, breaking a law anyone can check. |
| 2 | **Kepler I: the centre is a FOCUS.** | Offset by `e·a` — 20.6% for Mercury, whose orbit is visibly off-centre. Concentric circles lose it. |
| 3 | **Kepler II: equal areas.** | Speed varies as `(1+e)/(1-e)` between perihelion and aphelion — 1.52x for Mercury. No constant angular rate produces it. Kepler's equation `M = E − e·sin E` has no closed form; Newton converges in three passes at planetary eccentricities. |
| 4 | **Spin, which is unrelated to the orbit.** | Jupiter 9.93 h and Saturn 10.66 h are the fastest, and it shows in their SHAPE: flattening 0.065 and 0.098. |
| 5 | **Resonances, if there are moons.** | Io 1.769 d, Europa 3.551, Ganymede 7.155 — ratios 2.007 and 2.015, the 1:2:4 Laplace resonance. Drawn from the measured days, the resonance is a consequence, not a decoration. |
| 6 | **WHICH SCALE LIE YOU ARE TELLING.** | No diagram of a planetary system can be honest about distance and body size at once — Saturn's orbit is 2000 Sun-diameters across. Name the compression so that the numbers which ARE true stay checkable. |

## Failure signature

- evenly spaced periods → the law was not applied
- a body at the centre of its own ellipse
- constant angular rate on an eccentric orbit
