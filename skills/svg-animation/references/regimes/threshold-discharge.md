# Regime: threshold discharge

An event that builds, crosses a threshold, and releases: lightning, a crack propagating, a spark, a
dam failing, a queue collapsing.

The regime where the mechanism is most often drawn **backwards**.

## Must be known before drawing

| # | question | the measured defect that put it here |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Which direction the VISIBLE part travels.** | The old scene drew the bolt progressively downward. Wrong twice: the visible flash is the RETURN STROKE and it runs UP from the ground at about c/3 — 30 microseconds for a 3 km channel, far too fast to read as a draw at all. |
| 2 | **What the eye actually sees, which is the SEQUENCE.** | 3-4 return strokes down the same channel, 30-100 ms apart, mean ~60 ms; whole flash ~0.2 s. **That** is the flicker. It was never random opacity. The first stroke is brightest; later ones reuse the channel and are cleaner and dimmer. |
| 3 | **What chose the path, and in which direction.** | The stepped leader, descending at ~1.5e5 m/s in ~50 m steps. It is what branches, so branches point DOWNWARD. A cloud-to-ground flash with upward-pointing branches is the classic tell of a bolt drawn by hand. |
| 4 | **What carries no discharge.** | The branches carry no return stroke, so they fade first. |
| 5 | **Whether an exaggeration is needed, and stating it.** | The return stroke is a thousandth of one frame. Stretching it to 45 ms so the direction is visible is legitimate; leaving the stretch unstated is not. What is NOT negotiable is the direction. |
| 6 | **The rate of the event itself.** | Interval between flashes, and whether it is Poisson or driven. |

## Failure signature

- the discharge drawn in the direction the precursor travelled
- flicker as random opacity instead of a counted sequence of strokes
- branches pointing back along the discharge
