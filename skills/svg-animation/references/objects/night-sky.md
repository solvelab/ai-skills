# Night sky

regimes: `radiant-point-set`
view: a patch of sky with the horizon in frame, from a stated latitude — the pole's altitude equals
the latitude and the whole geometry follows from it

| fact | value | source |
|---|---|---|
| rotation | 15.041 deg/hour about the celestial pole | sidereal |
| **parallax** | **none. Stars are effectively at infinity.** | the category fact this regime exists for |
| magnitude scale | each magnitude 2.512x; counts rise as `10^0.6m`: ~4 brighter than m=1, ~1100 brighter than m=5, ~8700 to 6.5 | star catalogues |
| airmass | Kasten & Young: 1.02 at 80° altitude, 1.99 at 30°, 5.59 at 10°, 10.3 at 5° | atmospheric optics |
| extinction | 0.28 mag per airmass in V | photometry |
| scintillation | amplitude ∝ airmass^1.75; timescale 5-50 ms, i.e. tens of Hz | atmospheric turbulence |
| planets | do NOT twinkle — a disc of a few arcseconds averages the speckle out | optics |
| colours by class | O/B 155,176,255 · B 170,191,255 · A 202,215,255 · F 248,247,255 · G 255,244,234 · K 255,210,161 · M 255,190,130 | blackbody at each class's temperature |

assumed: the spectral mix of naked-eye stars. Weighted toward hot luminous ones, which is
qualitatively right — the true stellar population is mostly invisible M dwarfs — but the exact
fractions used are not from a cited catalogue.
