# Regime: advected field

Something carried by a flow while it grows and decays: cloud, smoke, steam, a fire plume, fog.

## Must be known before drawing

| # | question | the measured defect that put it here |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The level where it forms, and that it is SHARED.** | Cumulus base height by Espy: 125 m per °C of dewpoint spread. Every cumulus in a field condenses at the same level, so their bases lie on ONE horizontal plane. That aligned flat base is the most recognisable fact about a cumulus field, and the old scene had bases at whatever height each blob landed. |
| 2 | **What rule the boundary follows.** | Cumulus perimeter fractal dimension ≈ 1.35: self-similar, bubbles on bubbles. A smooth blob cannot read as cumulus at any size. |
| 3 | **Whether the boundary is a part or a CUT.** | A flat base is the condensation level slicing the bubbles off underneath, not a slab drawn under them. Drawn as a slab it protrudes as a shelf, a shape no cumulus has. |
| 4 | **Advection speed, and that it is one wind.** | Screen speed differs only by range, which is what parallax IS. This is legitimate parallax — unlike a starfield, these are at finite distance. |
| 5 | **Lifetime.** | A cumulus humilis grows and evaporates in 10-20 minutes. Grows fast, decays slow. |
| 6 | **Shading from depth, not from taste.** | White top, grey base: sunlight is scattered out on the way down through the depth of the cloud, so the underside is what is left. |
| 7 | **The puffing frequency, if it is a flame.** | `f = 1.5/sqrt(D)` Hz from the fire's DIAMETER — 17 Hz for a candle, 1.5 Hz for a 1 m pool fire. And a flame varies continuously; stepped flicker is a failing electrical contact, a different mechanism with the same name. |

## Failure signature

- bases at different heights → the shared level was not applied
- smooth outlines → no self-similarity
- a base wider than the cloud above it → the boundary was drawn as a part
