# Broadleaf tree

regimes: `growth-structure` (geometry) + `driven-oscillator` (motion)
view: profile; the sway is a bending in the wind's plane and profile is where it shows

| fact | value | source |
|---|---|---|
| whole-tree sway | 0.2-0.33 Hz; an open-grown tree with four branches measured 0.33 Hz; range across species 0.1-5 Hz | published free-vibration tests |
| branch modes | 2, 7 and 11 Hz | free vibration, sweet orange |
| damping | 10.6% with branches attached, 1.3% stripped bare | same source; the branches ARE the damper |
| radius at a fork | `r_child = r_parent·n^(-1/2)` — ×0.707 for two daughters, ×0.577 for three | Leonardo's rule, confirmed by many studies (exponent ~2 to 2.5) |
| drag exponent | U^1.3 to U^1.5, not U², because the crown reconfigures | Vogel |
| perimeter dimension | ≈ 1.35 | Lovejoy 1982 |
| leaf underside | markedly paler and matte: no palisade layer, often wax or hairs | botanical |
| wind strength anchor | Beaufort defines wind BY tree motion: 5 "small trees in leaf begin to sway", 6 "large branches in motion", 7 "whole trees in motion" | Beaufort scale |

derived: mean lean shared by compliance, `[1.5, 2.2, 3.2, 4.6, 6.6, 9.4]` deg per joint at Beaufort
6 — the trunk is thick and barely bends; almost all of a tree's give is the outer orders folding
back, which is what streamlining is.

assumed: the split of total lean across orders (compliance ratios derived from `qL⁴/EI`, not
measured on a tree).

unknown: published deflection angles for open-grown trees scatter too widely to anchor on. The
Beaufort description is used instead, and named as the anchor.
